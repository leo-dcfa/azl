Brushtail · how we test

How we test

A security check means letting someone look closely at your network, so you should know exactly what we will and won’t do. This page sets it out in plain words.

Most of these rules are built into Brushtail itself, so the tool refuses to break them. The one thing no tool can check is your signature, so nothing starts until it’s on paper.

See a sample report About Brushtail

Permission first

Nothing runs without your signature

Before we look at anything, someone able to authorise it, such as the owner or a director, signs a scope. Brushtail won’t start without one, and it refuses anything the scope doesn’t cover.

We never scan a business before it has signed. If you’re still deciding, we show you a sample report built from made-up data instead.

  • Who authorised it

    The scope names the person who signed, their role and the date they signed it.

  • Which networks

    Your networks, by the names your staff know, such as “Office” and “Guest Wi-Fi”. Only private office ranges are accepted, so internet addresses are never touched.

  • When

    A start and an end time. Before the window opens or after it closes, the tool refuses to run.

  • From where

    Before it sends anything, the tool checks our laptop is joined to a network named in the scope.

During the check

We look, we don’t touch

Brushtail looks and reports. These rules are written into the tool, not left to our good intentions.

01

One gatekeeper

A single check approves every address, before anything is scanned and again on everything the scan reports back. Anything outside the scope is refused.

Signed scope Private ranges only
02

Gentle by design

At most 50 packets a second by default, and never more than 300. We check common ports only (the 100 most common, by default), so your network and your team carry on as normal.

Rate cap Common ports
03

The public left alone

On guest or public Wi-Fi we count the devices we see but never probe them, and we don’t record their names or hardware addresses. Only the router and test devices you name are checked.

Guest Wi-Fi Counted, not probed
04

No attacks

We never guess passwords, run exploits, change settings or capture anyone’s traffic. The tool’s scan commands are fixed in its code, with no option to add others.

No passwords No exploits No capture
05

Lookups that say nothing about you

To find known flaws we send only product and version names, such as “OpenSSH 7.4”, to public vulnerability databases, and the public IDs of any flaws they match. Never an address, a device name or your business name.

NVD CISA KEV EPSS
06

Sealed evidence

Every command and its raw output is sealed with SHA-256 hashes, and your report carries each run’s seal. If any file changed afterwards, the hashes would stop matching, and you can check them yourself.

Start to finish

Before, during and after

  1. Before you sign

    1. A sample, not a scan

    While you’re deciding, you see a sample report built from made-up data. We don’t look at your network until you’ve signed.

  2. Before the visit

    2. The scope

    You sign a scope naming who authorised the check, the networks it covers and the time window. That’s all the tool will accept.

  3. On the day

    3. A dry run

    With our laptop on your network, a dry run confirms the scope, the window and the network, and shows the plan. It sends nothing.

  4. On the day

    4. The check

    One network at a time, staff network first, then guest Wi-Fi. Each run is sealed with its hashes as soon as it finishes.

  5. After the visit

    5. Report, then purge

    Once you accept the report, we purge the raw scan data, logs and device details. We keep the report, the findings, the scope and the hashes, so the record can still be checked.

Being honest

What this check doesn’t cover

A network check is one piece of your security, not all of it. Your report says the same, so nobody reads a clean result as more than it is.

See the limits section of a sample report

  • A point in time

    The report shows your network on the day we checked. A device added or a setting changed after we leave won’t be in it, which is what the re-test is for.

  • Devices that were off

    A laptop taken home, a PC that was switched off or a device asleep at the time won’t show up.

  • Email and cloud accounts

    Email, cloud accounts and the passwords that protect them sit outside your office network, so the network check doesn’t cover them.

  • Not every door

    We check common ports, not every port. We don’t test your internet connection from outside, and we don’t check IPv6.

FAQ

Questions about the rules

Why do you need a signed scope?

Because checking a network without permission isn’t a security check. In Australia, accessing or interfering with a computer system without authorisation can be an offence under the Criminal Code. The scope is your written permission, and it limits what we may look at and when. We can’t give legal advice, so if you’re unsure who can sign for your business, check with your own adviser.

Who should sign it?

Someone able to authorise it for the business, such as the owner or a director. The scope records their name, their role and the date they signed.

Can you check our network before we decide?

No. We never scan a business before it has signed. We’ll show you a sample report built from made-up data, so you can see exactly what you’d get.

Will you see our customers on the guest Wi-Fi?

Only as a number. On guest or public Wi-Fi we count the devices we see but never probe them, and we don’t record their names or hardware addresses. We test only the router and any test devices you name.

What do you keep after the check?

Once you’ve accepted the report, we purge the raw scan data, logs and device details. We keep the report, the findings, the scope and the hashes, so the record can still be verified later.

How can we tell the evidence hasn’t been changed?

Every command and its raw output is sealed with SHA-256 hashes, and the report carries each run’s seal. Ask us for the evidence pack and you, or your IT provider, can check the files against the hashes yourselves.

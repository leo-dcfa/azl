Why a check

Small offices have forgotten doors

Most small business networks grew one device at a time: a printer here, a camera there, a box someone set up years ago. Attackers look for exactly these. A check shows you what’s on your network before someone else goes looking.

  • Devices you didn’t know about

    An old storage box behind a cabinet, a camera nobody updated, a PC still sharing its files with everyone on the network.

  • Flaws already in use

    We flag software on the US government’s list of flaws attackers are exploiting right now, so those go to the top of the list.

  • A guest Wi-Fi that isn’t separate

    If a visitor on your guest Wi-Fi can reach your staff computers, the waiting room is inside your office network.

  • A list you can act on

    Every finding says what it is, where it is and what to do, in order of what to fix first. Your IT person or we can work through it.

What we check

Every device, every open door

Brushtail is our own tool, built for small offices. It runs from our laptop on your network and looks for the problems that most often let attackers in.

Known flaws

Software versions are checked against the US vulnerability database (NVD), CISA’s list of flaws being actively exploited (KEV), and exploit-likelihood scores (EPSS). Only product names and version numbers are looked up, nothing about your business.

NVD CISA KEV EPSS
04

Your Wi-Fi

We check that each network uses current encryption, that guests can’t reach your staff computers, each other’s devices or the router’s admin page. Visitors’ phones and laptops are counted and never probed, and their names aren’t stored.

Encryption Guest isolation Network separation

Your router

One discovery message, sent only to your router, shows whether devices on your network can open doors to the internet by themselves (UPnP).

How it works

From first chat to re-test

  1. Before the visit

    1. Talk

    What’s in your office, who looks after it, and what worries you. We can show you a sample report built from made-up data, so you see what you’ll get before we touch your network.

  2. Before the visit

    2. Scope

    You sign a scope that records who authorised the check, the hours it may run and the networks it covers. The tool refuses anything outside it.

  3. On the day

    3. Visit

    We come to your office and join the networks in scope with our own laptop. Nothing is installed on your machines, and your team keeps working.

  4. After the visit

    4. Report

    A report in plain English, ordered by what to fix first. It opens offline in any browser and prints to PDF for your records or your insurer.

  5. After the visit

    5. Fix

    Your IT person works through the list, or we walk through it with you and help where you’d like a hand.

  6. After the visit

    6. Re-test

    We check again and show you what’s fixed, what’s still open and anything new. Once you’ve accepted the report, we delete the raw scan data and keep only the report, the findings and their fingerprints.

Careful by design

Quiet, gentle and kept in scope

Brushtail looks and reports. No password guessing, no exploits, no recording of your traffic, and at most 50 packets a second by default. Every rule below is enforced by the tool itself.

  • One gatekeeper

    A single check approves every address, before anything is scanned and again on everything the scan reports back. Internet addresses are never touched.

  • Sealed evidence

    Every command and its raw output is kept with a fingerprint (a hash) of each file, so any later change to the record shows up.

  • Visitors left alone

    Devices on guest Wi-Fi are counted, never probed, and their names aren’t kept.

  • Tested to stay put

    The tool’s own tests fail if any of them tries to send a packet or reach the internet.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Will the check slow down or break anything?

It’s built to be gentle: at most 50 packets a second by default, and only common ports. It doesn’t guess passwords, run exploits or change anything, so your team can keep working while it runs.

Do we need to install anything?

No. We bring our own laptop and join your network for the visit. Nothing is installed on your computers.

What happens to the results?

The report and the sealed evidence are yours. The only thing that leaves your office during the check is a lookup of product names and version numbers against public vulnerability lists, which carries nothing about your business.

We already have an IT provider. Is this for us?

Yes. The report is written so your IT provider can act on it straight away, and a second pair of eyes is the point of a check. We’re happy to go through the findings with them.
